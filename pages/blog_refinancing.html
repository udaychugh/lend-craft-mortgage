Is Refinancing Right for You? How to Calculate Real Savings

SS

Sanjeev Sharma

Principal Mortgage Broker & Founder, Lend Craft Mortgage

Published June 12, 2026

Updated recently

With changing economic cycles and shifting cash rate decisions from the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA), keeping your home loan on autopilot can cost you thousands of dollars extra every single year. Many homeowners are loyal to their existing lender out of habit—a loyalty that banks often reward with what the industry calls a 'loyalty tax'.

However, refinancing isn't just about chasing headline interest rates. To make an informed choice, you must look at net savings after factoring in switching costs, loan term adjustments, and feature flexibility. Here is my practical guide on how to calculate if refinancing is genuinely right for you.

Step 1: Determine Your Rate Gap (The 'Loyalty Tax')

Lenders frequently offer discounted interest rates to attract new customers while keeping existing customers on higher standard variable rates. Check your latest home loan statement for your current interest rate.

If your current rate is more than 0.50% higher than what competitor lenders are advertising for your Loan-to-Value Ratio (LVR), it is almost certainly time for a loan review.

Example Breakdown:

On a $600,000 mortgage with 25 years remaining:

  • Current Rate (6.40%): Monthly repayment ≈ $4,012
  • Refinanced Rate (5.80%): Monthly repayment ≈ $3,790
  • Monthly Saving: $222 per month ($2,664 per year)

Step 2: Calculate the Upfront Refinancing Costs

Switching home loans isn't completely free, so any upfront costs must be weighed against your projected interest savings. The standard switching costs in Australia include:

  • Discharge Fee (Current Lender): $300 – $400 to release your mortgage deed.
  • Application & Setup Fee (New Lender): $0 – $600 (many lenders waive this during promotional cycles).
  • Government Mortgage Registration & Transfer Fee: Approx. $300 – $400 depending on your state.
  • Valuation Fee: Usually free, but occasionally $200 – $300 for commercial or unique properties.

Total Average Switching Cost: $700 – $1,200.

Step 3: Work Out Your Break-Even Period

The break-even point is the length of time it takes for your monthly savings to cover the total upfront cost of switching.

Break-Even Formula: Total Switching Costs ($1,000) ÷ Monthly Savings ($222) = 4.5 months.

If your break-even period is under 12 months and you plan to stay in the home for longer than that, refinancing is a clear financial win.

Step 4: Beware of the Loan Term Reset Trap

Here is a common mistake I warn clients about: if you have already paid off 5 years of a 30-year mortgage and you refinance into a new 30-year loan, your monthly repayments will drop significantly—not just because of the lower rate, but because you are stretching your debt over an extra 5 years.

Doing this means you will end up paying more interest in total over the life of the loan. When we structure a refinance for our clients at Lend Craft Mortgage, we adjust the new loan term back to match your remaining period (e.g., 25 years) or maintain your previous repayment level to accelerate your debt reduction.

Step 5: Tap Into Equity for Home Upgrades or Investment

Refinancing isn't only about lowering payments—it’s also an opportunity to access equity. If your property value has increased, you can top up your loan to fund home renovations, consolidate high-interest personal debt, or secure a deposit for an investment property.
