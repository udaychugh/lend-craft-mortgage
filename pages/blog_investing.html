Structured Finance Tips for Australian Property Investors

Building a multi-property real estate portfolio in Australia isn't just about finding high-yield suburbs or great capital growth potential. The true bottleneck for most investors is almost always borrowing capacity and loan structure.

Buy your first investment property incorrectly, and your borrowing capacity can grind to a halt—preventing you from acquiring property number two or three. Here are key structured finance strategies I advise savvy Australian property investors to implement from day one.

1. Avoid Cross-Collateralisation at All Costs

Cross-collateralisation occurs when a bank uses one property as security for loans on multiple properties. While major banks often set up loans this way because it’s convenient for their internal paperwork, it severely restricts an investor's flexibility.

If your properties are cross-collateralised and you decide to sell one property down the line, the bank can force you to use the sale proceeds to pay off loans on your other properties rather than releasing the cash to you.

The Smart Structure: Keep every property security standalone. Use separate loan facilities—and ideally different lenders—for each purchase so that growth in one property can be extracted as usable equity without triggering revaluations across your entire portfolio.

2. Separate Non-Deductible vs Deductible Debt

Under Australian tax rules (ATO), interest on loans used to purchase income-producing assets (like investment properties) is generally tax-deductible, whereas interest on your home owner-occupier loan is not.

To maximize tax efficiency:

  • Direct all extra cash flow, tax refunds, and rental profits into an offset account attached to your home loan (non-deductible debt).
  • Maintain interest-only repayments or minimal principal repayments on investment property loans while your owner-occupier debt exists.
  • Never mix personal funds with investment redraws; keep transaction accounts clean to simplify accountant tax reporting.

3. Strategic Use of Interest-Only (IO) Repayment Periods

Many portfolio investors choose Interest-Only repayment terms (typically 1 to 5 years) on investment loans. By not paying down the principal amount on investment debt, you preserve your monthly cash flow to quickly pay down your non-deductible home mortgage.

However, keep in mind that lenders apply a slightly higher interest rate margin to Interest-Only loans. As brokers, we calculate whether the tax and cash-flow benefits outweigh the rate premium for your specific stage of investing.

4. Utilize Standalone Usable Equity Cash Out Lines

As your existing properties grow in value, you can unlock usable equity without actually selling the asset. The safest way to structure equity release is by establishing a standalone top-up loan facility secured against Property A, placing those funds into a dedicated 100% offset account.

This creates an immediate liquid cash deposit ready for property B, ensuring you can act quickly when a purchase opportunity arises at auction or via off-market listings.

5. Stagger Lenders to Optimise Servicing Calculations

Every Australian lender uses a different servicing calculator. Some banks assess existing rental income more favorably, while others treat negative gearing tax benefits differently or apply varying benchmark interest rates when testing your debt capacity.

By strategically ordering which bank you use for loan #1, loan #2, and loan #3, a specialist mortgage broker can often extend your total borrowing capacity by hundreds of thousands of dollars compared to staying with a single lender.
