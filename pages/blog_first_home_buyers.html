5 Essential Tips for First-Home Buyers in Australia

Published June 18, 2026

Stepping onto the property ladder in Australia can feel like navigating a high-stakes obstacle course. Between shifting interest rates, strict bank serviceability tests, and property auctions, it’s completely natural to feel overwhelmed. But here’s the reality: with the right groundwork and structure, buying your first home is one of the most rewarding steps you'll ever take.

Over the past decade working with hundreds of Aussie buyers, I’ve noticed that those who succeed without unnecessary stress share a common playbook. Here are my 5 practical, field-tested tips to help you get your key in the front door smoothly.

1. Get Pre-Approval BEFORE You Start Weekend Inspections

It’s tempting to start browsing real estate portals and hitting open homes on Saturday morning. However, looking at properties without a pre-approval is like walking into a supermarket without your wallet—you don't truly know what you can afford, and you risk falling in love with a property out of reach.

A formal conditional pre-approval from a lender gives you:

  • A realistic borrowing ceiling based on your actual income and expenses.
  • The ability to make serious offers or bid at auction with confidence.
  • Faster final loan approval once your offer is accepted.

Pro Tip: Pre-approvals usually last 90 days. Keep your financial situation stable during this window—avoid changing jobs or applying for new credit cards until settlement is complete.

2. Leverage Government Schemes to Skip Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI)

Traditionally, if you have less than a 20% deposit, lenders require you to pay Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI)—which can add $10,000 to $30,000 to your upfront costs.

Fortunately, the Australian Federal Government’s First Home Guarantee (FHBG) allows eligible buyers to purchase with as little as a 5% deposit without paying LMI, as the government guarantees up to 15% of the loan value. Additionally, state-specific stamp duty concessions can save you tens of thousands in upfront taxes.

At Lend Craft Mortgage, we regularly review state and federal grant eligibility for our clients so they don't leave money on the table.

3. Budget for Hidden Purchase Costs (Beyond the Deposit)

Many buyers save up exact money for their deposit, only to be surprised by closing costs. When calculating your total savings target, remember to set aside funds for:

Conveyancing & Legal Fees

Expect around $1,200 – $2,500 to cover legal contract reviews and title transfers.

Building & Pest Inspections

Essential before purchase, usually costing between $500 – $800.

Government Transfer Fees

State land transfer and mortgage registration fees (approx. $300 – $800 depending on state).

Council & Strata Adjustments

Pro-rata rates adjustments paid at settlement (allow $1,000 – $1,500).

4. Audit Your Living Expenses 3 Months in Advance

Since the introduction of strict APRA underwriting guidelines, Australian banks examine your bank statements line-by-line using automated expense categorisers. High spending on food delivery, unused streaming subscriptions, or Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) accounts can reduce your calculated borrowing capacity.

Three months before applying for your loan, tidy up your bank statements: cancel unused subscriptions, close BNPL accounts (even if zero balance), and maintain regular savings habits.

5. Don't Just Shop for Interest Rates—Look at the Features

While a low interest rate is crucial, loan features like an 100% Offset Account or Redraw Facility can save you far more interest in the long run. An offset account acts as a transaction account linked to your mortgage; every dollar sitting in it offsets the principal loan balance on which interest is calculated daily.

Working with an independent mortgage broker allows you to compare products across 30+ Australian lenders to find the right balance of competitive rates and flexibility.
